Sun's elevationTerrain height in the Sun's directionSun clears the terrain (power)

Solar elevation is how high the Sun stands above a flat horizon. At the south pole it stays within about ±1.5° of the horizon, plus a few degrees more for high sites, so a panel lying flat gets almost nothing: at 1.5° it catches under 3% of the sunlight. That is why polar landers use vertical panels that turn to follow the Sun. The grey line is the terrain in the Sun's direction; power is only possible when the gold line is above it.
